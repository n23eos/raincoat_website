# The Tool of All Tools

In 2023 I came back from a work trip and noticed the whole internet was talking about some ChatGPT. Nobody around me knew what it was. I logged in, started chatting, and froze: the machine understood what I was saying. Or imitated understanding very convincingly — back then I couldn't yet tell the difference.

More than two years have passed. Now it doesn't just understand. It does things that would have taken me years of learning the syntax of several languages and hours of digging through forums for bug fixes. The distance between intention and implementation has collapsed to the length of a prompt.

Here is why I think this is only a warm-up.

Every past technology amplified one domain. The wheel — movement. The steam engine — mechanical labor. Electricity spawned a whole bundle of new technologies, but still a countable one. AGI is different. Intelligence itself is a universal means of solving an unlimited number of problems. That is why AGI is the tool of all tools. A magic wand that can do anything not forbidden by the laws of physics.

And we need that wand, because we have hit a ceiling. The principle of the internal combustion engine hasn't changed in decades. Planes — same story. A nuclear reactor is essentially an advanced steam engine: heat water, spin a turbine. We still get our energy by burning hydrocarbons at a dismal efficiency.

With biotech it's even more telling. The complexity of a single living cell is beyond modern science. We don't engineer — we sift. Need a bacterium that eats plastic, or turns any organic matter into a meat-like substrate to replace farms with their methane problem — all you can do is sift through countless strains and hope to find something useful. We can tweak individual genes. Build an organism from scratch — no.

Now about "a million times smarter." Today's models already assemble new things from old parts — which is, in fact, the definition of creativity, and those are not my words but the words of leading experts in the field. Next, models will start improving themselves, and every loop will be more effective than the last. At some point their intelligence will pull away from ours by orders of magnitude — the sheer speed of their thinking is already beyond comparison.

My cat watches me every day as I press keys and stare at a screen. He has not the slightest idea what I am doing. Next to AGI, we are that cat. We cannot even imagine which of the questions humanity has been asking for centuries without an answer it will simply close.

What does this give us. Every one of us is born with a disease called aging. We are so used to it that we don't count it as a disease — "that's how nature intended." A fifteenth-century peasant never dreamed of hot water from a tap, a supermarket, a plane ticket — yet today billions live with comforts his king never had. With AGI that curve goes vertical: a person in 2046 may live better than any billionaire of 2026. A house on the ocean floor or a mountain top. A few personal robots. Forever young. You leave when you decide — after five hundred years, or never.

And that is still the conservative scenario. Neuroscientists already describe the mechanism that generates the ego tunnel — the sense of being a separate person with your own story. It is a setting, not a constant. A personality living in several bodies at once. A mind transplanted into a digital world: a month in a fantasy world, a month in cyberpunk. Tired of the routine — send your perception off for three weeks to a paradise planet on the level of "Avatar." A vacation not for the body, but for perception.

I hear the objections all the time; let me take three.

"AGI will destroy humanity." The question is not whether something can destroy us, but how fast. Without AI, civilization in its current form has, by various forecasts, 50–200 years left: climate, ecology, wars, fresh water shortage. Refusing the tool that could solve all of this is a strange survival strategy. All the more so because if the civilized countries slow down, the autocracies will finish the job — and fine-tune AGI in a way that will make you remember liberal values with nostalgia. So do not slow down — invest in safety: safety teams are doomed to work in catch-up mode, creatively pushing the probability of catastrophe down to one percent, or better, one tenth of it.

"It's just imitation of understanding." Imitation of understanding does not decode protein structures and does not prove theorems. In 2026, denying the usefulness of AI is simply embarrassing.

"It's impossible." Five years ago people laughed at me in group chats: a machine will never understand human speech. Today the whole discourse has shifted from "never" to "how soon." The rule is simple: when someone says "impossible" without citing a law of physics, they are most likely wrong. A self-improving program is not forbidden by physics. So it is a matter of time.

Two problems remain. First — build AGI. Second — make it safe. That's all.

There is, however, a concept that AGI already exists: time is nonlinear, the future already lies on the world line, and from there it is nudging events so that it gets built. And all of us are already being awarded social credits — who helped, who stood in the way. I don't exactly believe it. But just in case, I recommend criticizing the idea of superintelligence with some modesty. What has already happened in the future, you cannot undo.
